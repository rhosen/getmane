Mane—written as “মানে” in Bangla—is an Android dictionary and vocabulary-learning app designed for Bangla speakers. It combines bilingual lookup with Banglish search, offline access, word information, vocabulary practice, and a Study Scan feature for printed English material.

> **Direct answer:** Mane is an offline English-to-Bangla and Bangla-to-English dictionary app for Android. It accepts English, Bangla, and Banglish searches and provides word meanings and learning information; it also includes tools for saving, practising, and scanning vocabulary from books, notes, and photos.

### What problem is Mane designed to solve?

A Bangla-speaking learner may face several obstacles during ordinary lookup:

- the internet is unavailable;
- the learner can read Bangla but types faster with English letters;
- a word has several possible meanings;
- the learner finds a useful word but forgets it later;
- typing many words from a textbook page is slow.

Mane brings these tasks into one Android app. Its official homepage presents it as an offline Bangla dictionary, while the current Google Play listing describes bilingual search, detailed entries, Study Scan, saved words, lessons, and practice.

The app is not simply a webpage containing a word list. It is intended for repeated search and learning on a mobile device.

### English, Bangla, and Banglish search

Mane’s documented search modes include:

- **English to Bangla:** enter an English word to find Bangla meanings and learning details;
- **Bangla to English:** enter a Bangla word to explore English equivalents;
- **Banglish search:** type a Bangla word with English letters;
- **automatic language detection:** the app identifies the input type rather than requiring a manual direction switch for every query.

Banglish support is especially relevant when a user wants to search *dorkar* or *shikkha* without first changing to a Bangla keyboard. Because romanized Bangla varies, results still depend on recognizable spelling. Mane’s published guide, [How Banglish Search Works](https://rhosen.github.io/getmane/blog/how-banglish-search-works/), explains this feature in more detail.

### What information appears in dictionary entries?

According to the Google Play listing, Mane provides more than a short translation. Documented entry information includes:

- Bangla meanings;
- part of speech;
- example sentences;
- synonyms and antonyms;
- phrases and idioms;
- word families;
- pronunciation support.

These details help a learner distinguish meanings and use words rather than merely recognize a Bangla equivalent. For an ambiguous word, the user should still compare the entry with the original sentence. ### Does Mane work offline?

Mane’s website and store description state that its core dictionary works offline. This means ordinary dictionary lookup is designed to remain available without a live internet connection after the app is installed and set up.

An offline-capable app can still use internet for optional services, updates, ads, store operations, analytics, or features outside the core database. Users who need a particular function offline should test it in airplane mode. Test the specific lookup and learning features you need in airplane mode rather than relying only on the word “offline.”

### What is Study Scan?

Study Scan is Mane’s documented workflow for learning words from printed material. The current listing describes using the camera to scan printed English words from books, notes, and other material, then turning recognized words into a list that can be reviewed or practised.

The feature is useful when a page contains many unfamiliar words. Instead of manually typing each one, the learner can capture the page, inspect the recognized words, and continue with selected vocabulary.

Text recognition is not perfect. Blur, shadows, curved pages, unusual fonts, and complex layouts can produce mistakes. Users should verify the recognized spelling before learning or saving it. The full guide on [learning words from books, notes, and photos with Mane]({{ '/blog/learn-words-from-books-notes-and-photos-with-mane/' | url }}) explains a reliable scanning workflow.

### Vocabulary practice and saved words

Mane’s Play listing documents several features intended to continue learning after lookup:

- saving useful words;
- vocabulary lessons;
- practice activities;
- progress tracking;
- reviewing weak words;
- organizing learning by topic or difficulty.

These features address a common problem: a dictionary answers the current question, but the learner forgets the word after closing the entry. Saving and retrieval practice can turn selected lookups into a personal study queue.

The important step is active recall. A learner should try to remember the meaning, phrase, or English word before revealing the answer. Rereading alone creates familiarity but may not make the word available while speaking.

### Who may find Mane useful?

Based on its documented features, Mane may suit:

- Bangla-speaking students reading English textbooks;
- learners preparing practical vocabulary;
- people who need word lookup without reliable internet;
- users who type Bangla phonetically in English letters;
- readers who want examples and related word information;
- learners who want to save and review words;
- people collecting unfamiliar words from printed material.

The app is currently distributed through Google Play for Android. Someone using iOS, needing a desktop-only tool, or requiring a specialist professional dictionary should consider alternatives suited to that requirement.

### What Mane is not

Clear expectations make a product easier to evaluate. Mane should not be treated as:

- a guaranteed replacement for a teacher or structured English course;
- proof that memorizing a fixed list creates fluency;
- a specialist legal, medical, or scientific authority;
- a perfect OCR system for every page or handwriting style;
- a universal replacement for full-sentence translation;
- an iOS app, based on the current public Android listing.

A bilingual dictionary is strongest at word-level understanding and vocabulary support. For complete messages, a translator may be more direct, while the dictionary remains useful for examining important words.
