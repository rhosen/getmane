Reading is one of the best places to meet useful vocabulary because every word appears in a real context. The difficulty is collecting those words without interrupting the reading session. Mane’s Study Scan feature is designed to reduce manual typing by recognizing printed English words from a camera image and organizing them for lookup and review.

> **Direct answer:** Mane helps you learn words from books, notes, and photos through Study Scan: capture clear printed English text, review the words detected from the image, check their English-to-Bangla information, save the useful items, and practise them later. The scan starts the workflow; accurate verification and repeated recall create the learning.

### What Study Scan does

The Mane homepage and Google Play listing describe Study Scan as a camera-based feature for printed English words. The process combines optical character recognition, or OCR, with dictionary and learning tools.

OCR is technology that detects text in an image and converts it into machine-readable characters. Google’s ML Kit documentation explains this general text-recognition process for mobile applications. In practical terms, the app tries to turn photographed text such as **environment**, **responsibility**, or **available** into searchable words.

Mane then connects recognized words to its dictionary and vocabulary workflow. The feature is not a camera translator for every visual situation; it is intended to make word collection from printed study material faster.

### A reliable scan-to-learning workflow

#### 1. Read the passage first

Do not begin by scanning every page. Read as much as you can and mark only passages containing words that block understanding or seem valuable for future use.

This preserves the purpose of reading. Constant lookup can break comprehension and encourage learners to collect far more words than they can realistically review.

#### 2. Capture a clear image

For better recognition:

- place the page on a stable surface;
- use even lighting;
- avoid shadows from your hand or phone;
- keep the camera parallel to the page;
- make the text large enough to read;
- focus before capturing;
- avoid reflections on glossy pages;
- capture a smaller section when the layout is crowded.

A sharp, straight image is easier to recognize than a distant photo of two curved pages.

#### 3. Review the detected text

OCR can confuse similar shapes, punctuation, columns, page numbers, or decorative fonts. Before saving a word, compare it with the printed source.

Common recognition problems include:

- *rn* read as *m*;
- *l* read as *I*;
- punctuation attached to a word;
- line-break hyphens treated as part of the spelling;
- headings and footnotes mixed with body text;
- names treated as general vocabulary.

The exact error pattern depends on the image and recognition system. Never memorize a scanned spelling without checking it.

#### 4. Select words that matter

A page may contain many recognized words, but not all deserve equal study. Prioritize words that are:

- important to the passage;
- repeated in the chapter;
- common across subjects;
- relevant to your exam, work, or daily life;
- partly familiar but difficult to use;
- connected to a topic you are actively learning.

Ignore obvious words, page labels, isolated names, and rare technical items unless they serve your current purpose.

#### 5. Open the dictionary entry

For each selected word, check:

- the sense used in the original sentence;
- part of speech;
- Bangla meaning;
- pronunciation;
- an example;
- a common phrase or related form.

Keep the original sentence available. A word such as *subject, current,* or *issue* may have several meanings. The article on [finding the correct Bangla meaning]({{ '/blog/find-correct-bangla-meaning-of-english-word/' | url }}) provides a context-checking method.

#### 6. Save a small, useful set

Mane’s listing documents saving scanned lists for offline review. A focused set is easier to remember than every unfamiliar item on the page. For one reading session, choose a manageable group and leave low-priority words for later.

Add a short note mentally or on paper: the source sentence, a phrase, or why the word matters. This makes the saved entry more meaningful.

#### 7. Practise retrieval

Review should not mean staring at the answer. Try to recall first:

- see the English word and recall its Bangla meaning;
- see the Bangla idea and produce the English word;
- complete a phrase;
- say your own sentence;
- explain the word in simple English.

Mane’s documented practice and weak-word review features can support this cycle. The general guide on [remembering English words without forgetting]({{ '/blog/remember-english-words-without-forgetting/' | url }}) explains why spaced retrieval and varied use are more effective than repeated rereading.

### Example: learning from a textbook paragraph

Imagine a science paragraph contains:

> Plants absorb water through their roots and transport it to other tissues.

You understand the topic but are unsure about **absorb**, **transport**, and **tissue**.

A productive workflow is:

1. scan the paragraph;
2. verify that the three spellings were recognized correctly;
3. ignore common words you already know;
4. inspect each entry in the sentence’s scientific context;
5. save **absorb water**, **transport something to**, and **plant tissue**;
6. explain the sentence in Bangla;
7. later reconstruct the English phrases from memory.

The phrases preserve usage better than three disconnected translations.

### Scanning a photo from another source

A saved photo or screenshot can be useful when it contains readable printed text, subject to the app’s current input options. Ensure you have the right to use the material and avoid scanning private documents unnecessarily.

For screenshots, crop away menus, captions, and unrelated text. For signs or labels, perspective and background texture can reduce recognition quality.

### What Study Scan cannot decide for you

OCR identifies characters; it does not determine which words are worth learning or which dictionary sense the author intended. The learner still needs to:

- choose important vocabulary;
- correct recognition errors;
- use sentence context;
- distinguish names from ordinary words;
- verify specialist meanings;
- practise the selected words later.

This is why scanning should be treated as a collection tool, not an automatic learning result.

### Build a weekly reading-and-review routine

A simple routine could be:

1. read for 15–20 minutes;
2. scan only one or two useful passages;
3. select a small set of words;
4. review them later the same day;
5. retrieve them again after a gap;
6. use several in writing or speech;
7. remove or deprioritize words you no longer need.

The correct daily number depends on available review time. The guide on [how many new English words to learn each day]({{ '/blog/how-many-new-english-words-per-day/' | url }}) helps set a sustainable target.

### Privacy and accuracy precautions

Avoid capturing confidential school records, personal messages, identity documents, medical papers, or workplace information unless you understand how the app processes data and have permission. Check the current privacy and data-safety information in the official store listing.

For academic quotations, names, formulas, and specialist terms, compare the recognized text directly with the page. A one-letter error can create a different word.

### Frequently asked questions

#### Can Mane scan a whole book at once?

The public feature description presents Study Scan as a way to capture printed words from study material, not as a book-digitization service. Use focused pages or sections and follow the app’s current limits.

#### Does scanning automatically teach the words?

No. It reduces collection and typing work. Learning still requires correct sense selection, recall, spacing, and use.

#### Should I save every unknown word?

No. Prioritize repeated, useful, and contextually important vocabulary. Too many low-value words can overwhelm review.

#### Can OCR make spelling mistakes?

Yes. Image quality, fonts, layout, and similar letter shapes can affect recognition. Verify every saved word against the source.

### Final takeaway

Mane’s Study Scan can shorten the path from printed page to vocabulary list, especially for learners who regularly study from books and notes. Use clear images, verify every recognized spelling, choose a small number of useful words, connect them to the original sentence, and practise retrieval after the scan.
