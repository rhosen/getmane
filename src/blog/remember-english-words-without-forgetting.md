Forgetting is not proof that you are bad at English. It is what happens when a word is encountered once, reviewed passively, or stored without enough connections. The solution is not to stare at the same list for longer. It is to practise bringing the word back from memory at useful intervals and in varied contexts.

> **Direct answer:** To remember English words, retrieve them without looking, review them over increasing intervals, learn them inside phrases and sentences, and reuse them in speaking or writing. Repeated exposure helps, but effortful recall is what tells you whether the word is actually available.

### Why rereading creates false confidence

When a word and its meaning are visible together, the pair feels familiar. Familiarity is not the same as recall. You may read **avoid = এড়িয়ে চলা** ten times and still fail to produce *avoid* when speaking.

Test memory before revealing the answer:

- What English verb means “এড়িয়ে চলা”?
- Complete: “Try to ___ making the same mistake.”
- Say one thing you avoid.

Each prompt requires retrieval. If you cannot answer, checking the word becomes feedback rather than passive exposure.

### Use spaced review instead of one long session

Reviewing a word several times in one sitting can improve immediate performance, but the later test is whether you can recall it after time has passed. Spaced review separates encounters.

A simple non-algorithmic schedule might be:

- later the same day;
- the next day;
- after three days;
- after one week;
- after two weeks;
- after one month.

Do not treat these intervals as fixed law. Easy words can move further apart; missed words should return sooner. The principle is to review near the edge of forgetting, not to repeat everything equally forever.

### Learn a phrase, not only a translation

Translations are useful bridges for Bangla-speaking learners, but a word becomes easier to remember when connected to a natural pattern.

Instead of:

> **responsible = দায়ী / দায়িত্বশীল**

Learn:

- **responsible for the project**;
- **Who is responsible for this mistake?**;
- **I am responsible for sending the report.**

The preposition *for* becomes part of the memory. The personal sentence adds a situation. During speaking, you can retrieve a chunk rather than assemble every word separately.

### Use more than one retrieval route

Strong memory has several paths. Practise a word from:

- English to Bangla;
- Bangla to English;
- a sentence with a blank;
- a picture or situation;
- a synonym or definition;
- an audio form;
- a personal question.

For **borrow**, prompts could include:

- What does *borrow* mean?
- How do you say “ধার নেওয়া”?
- Can I ___ your pen?
- What did you borrow recently?
- What is the difference between *borrow* and *lend*?

Varied prompts reduce dependence on one flashcard layout.

### Make examples personal but natural

A personal sentence is memorable because it links the word to existing knowledge. It should also reflect correct usage.

For *deadline*:

- generic: **The deadline is Friday.**
- personal: **My assignment deadline is next Sunday.**

For *prefer*:

- **I prefer tea to coffee.**
- **I prefer studying in the morning.**

Check a reliable dictionary when you are unsure about the grammar. A memorable incorrect sentence can also become difficult to remove.

### Meet words in meaningful input

Deliberate study starts the memory, while reading and listening strengthen it. When a studied word appears in a new article, video, lesson, or conversation, you learn more about its range.

Choose input where you understand most of the message. If every sentence contains many unknown words, you have too little attention for repeated noticing. Graded material, learner videos, and familiar topics create better conditions.

### Separate receptive and productive goals

You do not need to produce every word you understand. Mark vocabulary as:

- **receptive:** important to recognize in reading or listening;
- **productive:** important to use in speaking or writing.

Give productive words more demanding practice: Bangla-to-English recall, sentence creation, spoken answers, and correction. Receptive words may need fewer prompts, especially if they appear regularly in input.

This distinction controls workload and prevents an enormous review queue.

### Use sentence transformation

One sentence can create several retrieval events.

Base sentence:

> **She avoided answering the question.**

Transform it:

- I avoided answering the question.
- Why did she avoid the question?
- They are trying to avoid a delay.
- What should we avoid doing?

Transformation teaches form, grammar, and flexible use. It is especially effective for verbs, adjectives with prepositions, and phrasal verbs.

The [200 common English phrasal verbs with Bangla meaning](https://rhosen.github.io/getmane/resources/200-common-english-phrasal-verbs-bangla/) can be practised this way. Change the subject, tense, or object while preserving the phrasal verb’s meaning.

### Keep a small “difficult words” list

Do not repeatedly review every known word at the same intensity. Maintain a short list of items that you:

- confuse with another word;
- mispronounce;
- forget during speaking;
- use with the wrong preposition;
- understand only in one context.

Add a contrast or correction. For example:

- **borrow** = receive temporarily;
- **lend** = give temporarily.

Then practise both in one mini-dialogue. Difficult-word review should solve a specific problem, not simply increase repetition.

### A seven-day retention routine

#### Day 1

Learn five words with pronunciation, meaning, phrase, and personal sentence. Recall each without looking.

#### Day 2

Test all five in mixed order. Say one sentence aloud for each missed word.

#### Day 3

Read or listen to a short text. Notice whether the words or related forms appear. Use two in a short paragraph.

#### Day 4

No forced review unless a word appears naturally. This gap creates a more meaningful later test.

#### Day 5

Use Bangla or definitions as prompts and produce the English words. Mark difficult items.

#### Day 6

Speak for two minutes on a familiar topic while trying to use the target words naturally.

#### Day 7

Test again, keep weak words, and allow strong words a longer interval.

### Frequently asked questions

#### Why do I forget a word after learning it yesterday?

The first encounter creates a fragile memory. Forgetting after a delay shows that the word needs retrieval and new contexts. It is a normal part of learning, not a reason to restart the whole list.

#### Is writing a word ten times useful?

It may help spelling and attention, but copying without recall has limited value for meaning and speaking. Mix writing with closed-book retrieval and sentence use.

#### Should I use flashcards?

Flashcards can be effective when they require recall, use clear contexts, and follow spaced review. Avoid cards overloaded with every definition or cards you answer from visual position rather than knowledge.

#### How do I remember words while speaking?

Practise production under light time pressure. Answer familiar questions, retell short texts, and repeatedly use a small target set. Speaking access develops through speaking-like retrieval.

### Final takeaway

Words become durable when you repeatedly reconstruct them, not when you repeatedly look at them. Use spaced recall, phrases, personal examples, varied prompts, and meaningful input. Forgetting then becomes useful information about what to review next.
